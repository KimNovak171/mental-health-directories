# Teletherapy vs In-Person Therapy: Which Is Right for You?

The rise of teletherapy — therapy conducted via secure video call — has been one of the most significant developments in mental health care in recent years. For many people, it has removed genuine barriers: the commute, the scheduling difficulty, the discomfort of sitting in a waiting room. For others, the in-person experience remains irreplaceable.

If you are trying to decide between virtual and in-person care, here is an honest look at the advantages and limitations of each.

## What Is Teletherapy?

Teletherapy (also called telehealth, online therapy, or virtual therapy) involves meeting with a licensed mental health professional through a secure video platform rather than in a physical office. Sessions are typically the same length as in-person appointments — usually 45 to 60 minutes — and involve the same therapeutic methods.

Teletherapy platforms vary widely. Some therapists in traditional private practice offer video sessions through HIPAA-compliant tools like SimplePractice or Therapy Notes. Subscription-based services like BetterHelp and Talkspace have also grown significantly, though these operate differently from traditional therapy and have some important limitations worth understanding before you sign up.

## The Advantages of Teletherapy

**Accessibility.** This is the most significant advantage. People in rural or underserved areas often have limited access to local mental health providers. Teletherapy dramatically expands the pool of available providers. It is also a game-changer for people with mobility limitations, chronic illness, or disabilities that make travel difficult.

**Convenience.** Eliminating the commute makes it much easier to fit therapy into a busy schedule. A session during a lunch break or from a home office is possible in ways that an in-person appointment often is not.

**Comfort.** Some people find it easier to open up when they are in their own environment. The familiar surroundings of home can reduce the social anxiety that walking into a therapist's office might trigger — particularly for those whose anxiety is part of the reason they are seeking therapy in the first place.

**Continuity of care.** Teletherapy allows you to maintain your relationship with your therapist during travel, relocation (within the same state), or other circumstances that would otherwise interrupt treatment.

**Cost.** While rates vary widely, teletherapy sessions are sometimes less expensive than in-person appointments because therapists have lower overhead costs. This is not always the case, but it is worth comparing.

## The Limitations of Teletherapy

**Not appropriate for all presentations.** Teletherapy is generally not recommended as the primary treatment modality for severe mental illness, active suicidal ideation, psychosis, or serious eating disorders that require physical monitoring. If you are in crisis or have a complex presentation, in-person care — potentially including a higher level of care such as an intensive outpatient program — is likely more appropriate.

**Technology barriers.** A reliable internet connection, a private space, and a functional device are all prerequisites. For people who lack any of these, teletherapy may not be workable.

**Nonverbal information.** Experienced therapists gather important information from body language, posture, and other nonverbal cues that are harder to observe through a camera. Most skilled therapists adapt to this limitation, but it is a real one.

**Privacy concerns.** Having a confidential conversation requires a private space. For people who live with family members, roommates, or in small apartments, finding a genuinely private location for therapy can be challenging.

**Licensing limitations.** Therapists are licensed at the state level, which means they are generally only able to see clients who are physically located in the state where they are licensed. If you travel frequently or split time between states, this can complicate access to a consistent provider.

## What the Research Says

The evidence on teletherapy effectiveness has grown considerably. Multiple studies have found that video-based CBT for anxiety and depression produces outcomes comparable to in-person therapy. Specifically for anxiety disorders, teletherapy appears particularly effective — partly because the accessibility advantages mean clients are more likely to show up consistently.

For trauma treatment, some evidence-based modalities such as EMDR have been adapted for telehealth with positive results, though this remains an area of active study.

## Choosing Based on Your Situation

Here is a practical framework for deciding:

**Consider teletherapy if:** You have limited access to local providers, you have a busy schedule that makes in-person appointments difficult, you experience social anxiety that might be reduced by a familiar environment, or your presenting concern is anxiety, depression, stress, or relationship issues that fall within a moderate range.

**Consider in-person therapy if:** You are in crisis or your symptoms are severe, you have a complex diagnosis that benefits from more thorough clinical observation, you have tried teletherapy and found it unsatisfying, you do not have access to a private or quiet space at home, or you simply prefer the structure and ritual of an office appointment.

**Try both and see.** Many therapists offer either format, and many clients find that what works best becomes clearer after trying. There is no rule that says you have to pick one and stick with it.

## A Note on Subscription-Based Platforms

Apps like BetterHelp and Talkspace have made it very easy to get matched with a therapist quickly. However, these platforms have faced criticism for their matching processes, therapist workloads, and messaging-based models that may not replicate traditional therapy effectively. If you choose to use one of these platforms, be aware of what you are getting — and be proactive about evaluating whether the care you are receiving is genuinely meeting your needs.

For most people, working with a licensed therapist in private practice — whether in-person or virtually — through traditional channels provides the most reliable therapeutic experience.
